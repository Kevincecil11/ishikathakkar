# How the site is organised (and why)

## The one-glance rule
Recruiters/clients give a portfolio ~7 seconds on first view (Ladders eye-tracking, 2018), and 10–30 s on a homepage before deciding to go deeper. So the hero alone has to answer: **who is she, what does she do, how much of it.**

## One axis: discipline, with colour = discipline
A multidisciplinary person reads as "jack of all trades" when work is listed by date or employer. Grouping by **what kind of work** shows depth in each area. Every project belongs to exactly one lane, and the lane colour follows it everywhere (cards, results panel, next-project band).

## Homepage, top to bottom
1. **Hero**: huge name → "I build brands, communities and stories." → two headline roles → four coloured lane cards (count + projects). *This is the at-a-glance breakdown.*
2. **About (short)**: portrait, her line "I think in stories before I think in strategy.", 5 role rows, link to full story.
3. **At a glance**: 5 proof numbers (42%, 115k, 3 awards, ₹4L+, 4 editions), each linking to its project.
4. **Work, by discipline**: one lane per discipline, image cards.
5. **Brands & stages**: logo wall (trust).
6. **Say hello**: contact.

## Project page = progressive disclosure
Opening a card reveals the full work: title → one-line summary → Role / Organisation / Discipline → hero image → Brief / Approach / Outcome → What I did → **results panel** (lane colour) → gallery → what I took from it → connected work → next project.

## Labels
Plain, descriptive labels over clever ones (NN/g information scent): "Brand & Marketing", not "Stories that sell".
